# Willingness-to-Pay Interview Script — Breeders (China)

_For Pet Health OS · use with catteries/kennels (猫舍/犬舍). ~30–45 min per interview._

The goal of these interviews is **not** to sell, and **not** to ask "would you pay
X?" in a vacuum. It's to (1) confirm the problem is real and expensive, and
(2) extract honest price signal. Numbers only mean something once the pain is real.

---

## How to use this (read before every interview)

**Principles (from _The Mom Test_):**
- **Talk about their life, not your idea.** Ask what they do *today* and what
  happened *last time* — past behavior predicts payment; opinions about the
  future do not.
- **Don't pitch until the end.** If you describe the product early, you'll get
  politeness, not truth.
- **Dig into specifics and numbers.** "It's a pain" is useless. "I lost 3 sales
  last month and refunded ¥12,000" is gold.
- **Compliments are a warning sign, not a win.** "Cool idea!" = you learned
  nothing. Look for *commitment* (time, money, reputation, referrals).
- **Shut up and listen.** Aim to talk <30% of the time. Silence pulls out truth.

**Logistics:**
- **Who:** active breeders who *sell* animals. Mix cat (猫舍) and dog (犬舍);
  mix small (1–2 queens/dams) and larger/cattery-as-business operators.
- **How many:** ~10–15 before drawing conclusions; stop when answers repeat.
- **Recording:** ask permission; otherwise take verbatim notes (their words, not
  your paraphrase).
- **One rule:** never say a price first. Let them say numbers; you echo and probe.

## Section 0 — Warm-up & consent (2 min)

- Thanks for the time — I'm researching how breeders manage animal health and
  sales, I'm **not selling anything today**, just learning. Mind if I take notes?
- Tell me about your cattery/kennel — what do you breed, and how long have you
  been at it?

## Section 1 — Their operation (context + the numbers that set the ceiling) (5 min)

> _Why: pricing scales with their revenue and volume. A breeder selling ¥30k
> kittens has very different WTP than a hobbyist._

- How many breeding animals do you have right now? How many litters a year?
- Roughly how many animals do you sell in a year?
- What's the typical price range for one of your animals?
- Where do you find buyers? (WeChat, 朋友圈, Douyin/抖音, 闲鱼, offline, referrals…)
- Is this your full-time business or a side passion?

## Section 2 — How they manage health today (behavior + pain) (8 min)

> _Why: this is the "health log" job. Find the current workaround and its cracks._

- Walk me through how you track an animal's health right now — from birth to sale.
  (Probe: notebook? WeChat notes? Excel? memory? a vet system?)
- Show me, if you can — what does it actually look like? _(Ask to see it.)_
- When was the last time an animal got sick? Walk me through exactly what happened
  and what you did.
- What's the most annoying or risky part of keeping these records?
- Have you ever lost or forgotten health info you wished you'd had? What happened?
- How do you keep track of vaccines, deworming, and vet visits across many animals?

## Section 3 — The sale & the handoff (the passport job — your wedge) (8 min)

> _Why: this is where your differentiation lives. The 星期猫/星期狗 fear is the
> emotional core — let them raise it; if they don't, ask gently._

- When you sell an animal, what does the buyer ask you about its health/lineage?
- What do you give the buyer today? (Vaccine card? Pedigree? Verbal promises?
  A WeChat message? Nothing?)
- Do buyers ever worry the animal might get sick after they take it home? How do
  you reassure them? _(Listen for 星期猫/星期狗.)_
- Have you ever had a buyer come back claiming the animal was sick — a dispute,
  refund, or a bad review? Walk me through the worst one.
- How much does your reputation depend on buyers trusting your animals are healthy?
- After the sale, do buyers ever message you for health/care advice? How often?

## Section 4 — Quantify the pain (turns pain into ROI) (5 min)

> _Why: their own numbers become the value anchor you compare price against._

- Roughly how much time per week goes into health records and answering buyer
  questions?
- In the last year, how much money would you estimate you lost to: sick animals,
  refunds/returns, or sales that fell through over health/trust concerns?
- If a tool reliably prevented even one of those, what's that worth to you?
- What do you currently *pay* for, in running the cattery? (Vet, software,
  marketing/ads, registration, grooming…) Ballpark monthly?

## Section 5 — Concept reaction (now you can show it — briefly, neutrally) (4 min)

> _Keep it to two sentences. Don't oversell. Watch their face._

- "Some breeders are starting to use a tool where you log each animal's health in
  plain language, an AI helps flag problems early and prepare notes for the vet,
  and when you sell, the animal's full health history transfers to the buyer as a
  shareable record they keep for life." What's your honest first reaction?
- Which part — if any — would actually matter to *you*? Which part is noise?
- Is there anything you'd need it to do before you'd consider using it?

## Section 6 — Willingness to pay (only after the above) (8 min)

Frame pricing as **per month, per cattery** (you can also probe annual). Let them
answer in their own currency/units; echo their number and ask "why that number?"

### 6a. Van Westendorp (four questions — ask in this order)

1. At what **monthly price** would this be **so expensive you wouldn't consider
   it**? （多贵你就完全不会考虑了？）
2. At what monthly price would it start to feel **expensive, but still worth
   considering**? （多少钱开始觉得贵，但还值得考虑？）
3. At what monthly price would it feel like a **good deal / good value**?
   （多少钱你会觉得很划算？）
4. At what monthly price would it be **so cheap you'd doubt it's any good**?
   （多便宜会让你怀疑它靠不靠谱？）

_(These four plotted across many interviews reveal the acceptable price band.)_

### 6b. Gabor-Granger (test concrete points — find the demand curve)

Ask "Would you pay **¥___/month** for this, given what we discussed?" Start in the
middle and move up/down based on answers. Suggested ladder:

> ¥299 → ¥599 → ¥999 → ¥1,499 → ¥1,999 → ¥2,999 / month

At each "yes," go higher; at each "no," ask **why** and what would justify it.
This directly tests the ¥2,000 anchor without you ever proposing it.

### 6c. Test the model, not just the number

- If it were a flat monthly fee that covered up to **50 animals**, then a small
  fee per extra animal beyond that — does that structure make sense to you? What
  feels fair per extra animal?
- Would you rather pay **monthly or yearly**? (Yearly often signals commitment.)
- Would you pay **per animal sold** instead of a subscription? (Tests an alt model.)
- Who decides on a purchase like this — just you, or a partner/spouse?

## Section 7 — Commitment & advancement (the real signal) (3 min)

> _Talk is cheap. These questions separate "nice idea" from "take my money."_

- If this existed today and did exactly what we described, would you start using
  it this month? What would hold you back?
- Would you be willing to join a **paid pilot** at an early-bird price? _(If they
  hesitate at any price, the earlier enthusiasm was politeness.)_
- Can I follow up when there's an early version for you to try?
- Who else — other breeders — should I talk to? Could you introduce me?

## Section 8 — Wrap (1 min)

- Anything about managing your animals' health or selling them that I *didn't*
  ask about but should have?
- Thank them. Confirm follow-up. Ask for the referral introductions concretely.

## What to listen for

| 🟢 Strong signal | 🔴 Weak / misleading |
| --- | --- |
| Describes a specific recent loss with numbers | "It's generally a hassle" (vague) |
| Already cobbling together a workaround (Excel, notebook, WeChat) | "I just remember everything, no problem" |
| Raises 星期猫/星期狗 / disputes unprompted | Only excited *after* you pitch |
| Offers to pre-pay, pilot, or introduce others | "Cool! Good luck!" with no commitment |
| Gives a price band without flinching | Won't name any number |

## Aggregate analysis (after ~10 interviews)

- **Acceptable price band:** overlap of Van Westendorp curves across breeders.
- **Demand curve:** % who said yes at each Gabor-Granger price point → revenue-
  maximizing point.
- **Segment splits:** do larger/dog vs cat/full-time breeders cluster at higher
  WTP? (May justify Lite vs Standard tiers.)
- **Reality check:** % who gave a *commitment* (pilot/referral), not just a price.

## Do / Don't

- ✅ Let them say the first number. ❌ Never anchor with your price first.
- ✅ Ask about the last real incident. ❌ Ask hypothetical "would you…" alone.
- ✅ Probe "why that number?" every time. ❌ Accept a number without the reason.
- ✅ Treat referrals/pilots as the truth. ❌ Treat compliments as validation.
- ✅ Interview both cat & dog, big & small. ❌ Generalize from 2–3 chats.
